## What changed versus the screen
- The screen and the title check treated this as a dated 1920 twin needing a medium rehab, with a possible senior Wildcat mortgage (worst case $192,500 surviving). Both are wrong:
  1. **It's already renovated.** HBCB Properties LLC (Margate FL; Wilmington DE mail drop) bought it from the Hedgman/Thornton estate for $225,000 on 5/31/2024 and gutted it. It hit the MLS 5/29/2026 at $499,900 as a 4 bed / 3.5 bath, "2,800 sf" (with finished basement) luxury twin. OPA re-graded it to interior/exterior condition 2, 4 bed / 3 bath, central air, and raised the 2027 value from $234,700 to $446,500.
  2. **Title is clean.** The plaintiff is LHOME Mortgage Trust 2025-RTL2 on the 3/26/2025 Kiavi mortgage. The Wildcat purchase loan was released 4/2/2025 (PhilaDox 54408619, mis-indexed to "4800 Dexter Dr" but linked to the Wildcat mortgage 54314048). The Fire Opal 2nd was satisfied the same minute. No other liens against HBCB at the Recorder. The "Subject To Mortgage" notice flag is boilerplate (see title-liens/README).
- **No permits.** L&I has no building, plumbing, electrical or mechanical permit for the renovation, ever. New baths (3 added), a basement bath, central air and a rear patio were all done without permits.

## Why it's being sold
- HBCB is a small out-of-state flipper (one Philly property). Financing: Wildcat hard-money purchase loan + Fire Opal gap 2nd (6/2024), refinanced into a Kiavi rehab/bridge loan (3/2025), which Kiavi sold into the LHOME 2025-RTL2 securitization (11/2025).
- The flip ran long. The loan defaulted by early 2026 (complaint 2/12/2026, $545,562), before the house was even listed. HBCB was personally served in Florida 3/14/2026 and never answered: default judgment 5/12/2026, damages raised to $571,979.30 on 5/20, writ 6/2/2026.
- Listed 5/29/2026 at $499,900; cut to $479,900 (7/7), $459,900 (8/28), $399,900 (9/16), and flagged **short sale**. With $572k owed, any sale at $400k needs LHOME to eat ~$200k.
- The lender continued the 9/1 sale to 11/3 (9/29 notice), most likely to give the short sale time.

## Who is living there
- **Probably no one.** It was staged for the listing (matching staged furniture, no personal items) and has been on the market 149+ days. Investor owner with an out-of-state address. No rental license. No homestead.
- Caveats: the process server personally served "unknown occupants/tenants" at the house on 3/30/2026, so someone was there that day (likely a contractor finishing the flip, 2 months before listing). A vacant, staged, nicely finished house that sits through the winter is a squatter target in Philadelphia. Treat as **70% vacant at deed / 30% someone in it** (squatter, a tenant HBCB puts in for cash flow, or a "buyer" let in early on a failing short sale).
- Drive-by check: lockbox on the door, staging still visible through the windows, for-sale sign, lights on timers, mail piling up, utilities (PECO/PGW meters).

## Stay / postponement odds (my estimate)
- **Short-sale approval before 11/3: the big risk.** 179 saves and 1,446 views at $399,900, so it is getting attention. If a buyer is under contract and LHOME approves, LHOME postpones the sheriff sale (they need until 3 pm 11/2, or can pull it via the B4A attorney portal during the auction). A previously continued sale plus an active short sale: **~35-40% chance it's postponed again**.
- **Bankruptcy: low (~5%).** The LLC never appeared in the case and its owner is in Florida. An LLC Chapter 11 is possible but rare for a one-house flipper.
- **Plaintiff bidding:** posted minimum is only $18,400 (costs). The lender may still bid on B4A to protect a ~$400k asset, or set its own floor via the attorney; watch for an early high bid from the plaintiff's account.
- Net: **~55-60% the sale happens on 11/3.**
