## 5. What this means for lien position
- The foreclosing party is the lender/servicer on the 2019 purchase-money mortgage (INFERRED, not VERIFIED: the docket does not state the mortgage date; basis is that the judgment is $329,601.29 at a 4.5% rate against a $237,000 purchase and the plaintiff is a first-lien servicer. A $259,621.85 complaint amount in Nov 2022 is plausible for a 2019 purchase loan that was ~3 payments of principal paid but several years of arrears/escrow/fees, but is also large enough that it could include a HELOC; this should be confirmed by reading the complaint/mortgage identification in the paid PDF "Complaint for filing.pdf" or the writ/3129 notice, ~$5 convenience fee plus doc charges, or by calling Pincus Law Group).
- If NewRez's mortgage is the 2019 MERS/Mortgage America first mortgage (the 2026-04-29 assignment to JPMorgan Chase may relate to it): this is a **FIRST-LIEN foreclosure with a judgment of $329.6k (plus 4.5%/yr), which wipes out all junior mortgages (Figure/Green Lake HELOC) and judgment liens that are junior** at sheriff's sale. Buyer takes subject only to: unpaid taxes/water/sewer municipal liens (city liens generally are paid from sale proceeds or survive depending on priority; confirm tax status), and any senior interests. The 2008 HSBC mortgage (unsatisfied of record) is senior in recording order to the 2019 mortgage if truly outstanding; however, the 2019 purchase money loan almost certainly paid it off at closing (unrecorded satisfaction is a title cleanup issue, not an economic lien; title company would show payoff). Get a title search/order Philly sheriff-sale title.
- If instead NewRez were foreclosing on a junior position, the first would survive: NOT supported by the docket (NewRez sued alone, as a servicer d/b/a Shellpoint, claims $330k, far above what a HELOC on this house plausibly is, and Figure HELOCs are typically $20-150k).
- Because no junior lienholder, Figure/Green Lake Trust, or tenant is joined as a party to this docket, their interests are extinguished or not extinguished depending on whether they were named; under PA practice a junior mortgagee that is NOT a party is still divested of the lien by a judicial sale on a senior mortgage when the senior has priority (but must be given notice under Rule 3129.2; the "3129.2 with exhibits" affidavit was filed 29-NOV-2025). The Sheriff's "no further notice to lienholders" order at the 8/25/2026 postponement is consistent with that.
- Opening bid math for the Nov 3 sale: plaintiff bids up to its judgment (~$330k + 4.5% interest + costs, approx $345-350k by 11/3/2026). A third-party bidder must exceed the plaintiff's bid or be at/above any property's market value; if comps are materially below ~$345k the plaintiff will credit-bid and take title (REO), with pre-sale purchase from the owner unlikely to clear. The 1471 E Wilt result ($176k bought by the plaintiff's law firm against a $222k judgment) shows how these sales settle: lender took it back at a number below judgment.
- Risk flags before bidding: (1) wrong-address (5616 Thomas Ave) in the 8/25/2026 postponement order; (2) a pending/new bankruptcy filing before sale; (3) plaintiff may postpone again (it has done so five times; the sale has been continued since 2025); (4) possible open water lien 2207W22009205; (5) possible occupant (case type "owner occupied"; returned mail suggests vacant, INFERRED); (6) complaint text not read.
