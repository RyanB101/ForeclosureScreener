## The property

- 2-story brick row, **700 sf, 2 bed / 1 bath**, built ~1920, 13 x 39 lot, basement, hot-water or steam heat (OPA code H), no central air. One of ten identical 700 sf rows on the 2700 block of Reno, a small street between 27th and 28th, 70 ft west of Pennock St, south of Girard in the Fairmount/Art Museum area.
- **Condition: OPA rates exterior and interior 4 (below average).** No permits ever on record; same owners since 1978. Expect a 1970s-80s interior: original kitchen and bath, old wiring and boiler, possibly old windows and roof coating. Medium rehab is the right default; heavy is the downside.
- No L&I violations or cases, no rental license.

## Who lives there

- **Konstantinos H. and Giannoula Katseftis, owners since 1978**, almost certainly elderly (48 years of ownership).
- Evidence they live there: both were **personally served at the house** in May 2024 and again in May 2026; their mailing address is the house; the court coded it residential owner-occupied; the city has given them the **long-time owner-occupant (LOOP) assessment freeze** since at least 2015 (exempt $111,600 on a $285k value), which requires owner-occupancy and an income limit.
- Family nearby: Konstantinos M. and Joan Katseftis (likely relatives) have owned 2801 and 2803 Poplar St, one block away, since 1988-89 (city value $1.14M combined). Other relatives owned 2319 Parrish until 2025.
- So: an elderly, income-limited couple in their home of 48 years, with family close by. Assume occupied.

## Why it's being sold

- Citizens has been their lender since 2007. They refinanced in 2007, 2008, 2015 and **June 2019**. The 2019 Citizens loan is the only open mortgage and is the one being foreclosed. The complaint was $240k in May 2024, so it was probably a $220k to $240k cash-out refi on a house then assessed at $233k.
- The judgment is **$275,615** (March 2026), and with interest the payoff is now about $290k, above the house's as-is value (~$265k). **They have no equity**, so a refinance or a retail sale doesn't rescue them; only a loan modification, a family buyout, or a short sale does.
- Court history: missed the first conciliation round (Sept 2024), re-engaged, and by Feb 2025 a **loan modification was agreed pending their signature**. Talks ran through Nov 2025 with no deal. Default judgment March 2026, writ April. **Giannoula's own petition postponed the July sale**, then Citizens continued October to November and moved the court on Oct 22 to push it to Dec 1.

## Business plan

**Plan A: flip (recommended).**
1. Win at $130k to $160k on Dec 1 (if it runs). Wire 10% + 1.5% premium the next business day and 90% within 15 days.
2. **Possession.** This is an elderly couple in their long-time home with family a block away. Approach the family right after the deed records with a generous cash-for-keys offer ($10k to $15k plus moving help); it is faster and far better than ejecting elderly owners. Budget 8 months from deed; ejectment as a fallback.
3. **Rehab, about $55k to $65k over ~3 months (medium, dated to modern):** new kitchen and bath, electrical panel and wiring as needed, boiler service or replacement plus mini-splits for cooling, refinish floors, paint, windows if original, roof coat and front repointing (+$8k). Option: add a half bath for the $425k upside (+$12k). Pull permits.
4. **List at $399k to $415k**, against the Newkirk and Judson sales; expect about $395k.

**Plan B: hold.** Doesn't work: about $1,900 to $2,100 a month in rent on a 700 sf 2/1 supports a bid of only about $30k.

**Plan C: wholesale.** Assigning to a flipper supports about $180k to $190k in the base case, a floor under the exit if possession drags.
