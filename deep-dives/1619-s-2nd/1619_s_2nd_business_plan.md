## Why the sale is collapsing: owner death and heirs

- **The owner is dead.** John T. Bowers, Sr. died at home on April 22, 2024, age 62. Source: obituary from Rachubinski and Rogers Funeral Home, details in `sources/owner_death_check.md`. He leaves his wife Marie (née Lowther) and two children, Stephanie Masci and John T. Bowers, Jr.
  - He is the owner: the mortgage was signed "John T. Bowers", the 2019 satisfaction on 2134 S Front names John and Marie, and the 2020 deeds of his other houses add Stephanie and John Jr.
- **He was a small Pennsport landlord.** At least 10 licensed rentals were in his name or JTB Realty. In 2020 he deeded four houses into joint names with both children, but 1617 and 1619 S 2nd stayed in his sole name. Those two pass through the estate.
- **Who was sued:**
  - Only "Stephanie Masci, in her capacity as administratrix of the estate", served in person at 2233 S Front in January and August 2026.
  - The heirs (Marie, Stephanie and John Jr.) were not named individually, and neither were "unknown heirs".
  - Pennsylvania lets a lender sue the personal representative alone (Pa.R.C.P. 1144), but only if letters were really granted.
- **Why the plaintiff is backing out:** the 16-page motion would say ($4 on fjdefile.phila.gov). I did not buy it. The most likely reasons, all guesses:
  1. There was no valid grant of letters to Stephanie, so the judgment is against someone without capacity.
  2. An heir or the widow had to be named or noticed.
  3. A pre-foreclosure notice defect (Act 6/91) on a deceased borrower's loan.

  Reaffirming the mortgage lien is the standard step when a lender discontinues and plans to refile.
- **The debt is small for the asset.** The complaint was $82,643 and the judgment $92,898, against an as-is value of about $320k to $345k. The estate has roughly $230k+ of equity. A refiled case may never reach sale, because the estate can refinance, pay off, or sell the house. That is another reason to treat this as a watch item, not a bid.

## The property

- 3-story masonry row converted to apartments, **licensed for 3 units from 2005 until the license lapsed 2/28/2022**, not owner-occupied. Built 1915. Lot 16 x 70.
- 1,560 sf, so each floor plate is about 520 sf: three small 1-bedroom or studio units.
- Zoned RM1. Hot-water heat, full basement, no central air. OPA condition 4 / 4 (average).
- **No permits, no L&I violations, ever.** No MLS listing since at least 2001, and no photos anywhere (`sources/zillow_redfin.md`). **The interior is unknown.**
- City value $320,900 (2027). Taxes about $4,500 a year. No homestead.
- Next door, 1617 S 2nd is also in the estate. It is a 3-story converted apartment building of 1,870 sf with its own open 2005 Wachovia mortgage and is not in foreclosure.

## Who's likely living there

Assume it's occupied: Ryan's default rule, and the evidence points that way.

1. **Most likely: tenants of the Bowers family.** It was a licensed 3-unit rental for 16 years. The family still owns and manages the Front St houses, and the estate needs the rent. The license lapsing in 2022 probably means it wasn't renewed, not that it emptied. Unlicensed tenants are still protected:
   - The Protecting Tenants at Foreclosure Act (PTFA) gives 90 days' notice or the rest of the lease.
   - In Philadelphia you need a rental license and a certificate of rental suitability to evict.

   Budget three separate households.
2. **Possible: a family member** in one unit. Stephanie lives at 2233 S Front, and the widow's address is not on record.
3. **Less likely: vacant.** The 311 history is thin (2019, 2020), with no vacant-property violations.

**Check before any future bid:** count the doorbells, meters and mailboxes; look for lights in each floor; and ask the neighbors at 1615 and 1613.

**Possession budget used:** 9 months after the deed and $15k as the base case (cash for keys of about $4-5k per unit, or Municipal Court with a license). The slow case is 12 months and $25k.

## Business plan (for a refiled sale)

**Plan A: de-convert to a single-family house and flip.**
1. Buy at $130k to $150k, all cash.
2. During the 12-month deed wait, get the use change from 3 units to 1 family (a reduction, which RM1 allows), then drawings and permits.
3. At the deed: give the three households PTFA notices, offer cash for keys, and license the building if removal goes to Municipal Court.
4. Rehab, about 3 months and $128k:
   - one kitchen, two full baths;
   - remove the two upper kitchens;
   - refinish floors, paint, new electric panel;
   - mini-splits or a high-velocity system;
   - keep the boiler if it tests OK.
5. List at $510k to $525k against 1537 S 2nd ($500k), expecting about $500k.

**Plan B: light refresh, keep 3 units, sell or hold.**
- Three units at about $1,400 each is $4,200 a month (Rent Zestimate $2,008 for the whole building; SAFMR 1BR $1,630).
- Sold to an investor at about $470k, max bid $215k.
- As a hold, it only works at bids under about $100k (model hold max $101k).

**Plan C: buy direct from the estate before any refiled sale. This is the best angle.**
- The estate (administratrix Stephanie Masci, 2233 S Front St) owes about $95k to $100k on a $320k+ building and has 1617 S 2nd next door with its own old mortgage.
- An estate that let the loan go to judgment, and skipped conciliation, may prefer a quick cash sale.
- An offer of about $250k to $275k as-is would give it about $150k+ net. On the medium plan at $500k resale, no deed wait and buyer-handled tenants, that is still roughly a $50k+ profit after 8%.
- If you want it pursued, a buyer's attorney should confirm the letters of administration before signing.
